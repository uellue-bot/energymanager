# Weather normalisation of weekly cost

Question: is the planned comparison of control strategies a proven method or our own invention? The method: fit on the baseline weeks cost ~ a + b·HDD + c·PV + d·HDD·PV, with HDD = Σ over the week's days of max(0, T_base − daily mean outdoor temperature), T_base fitted (about 12–13 °C with temperature alone, a flat optimum around 13–18 °C with the PV terms), PV = the week's measured PV yield; then plot each week's actual cost against the model's expected cost for that week's weather, and the saving = expected − actual over time. On 98 baseline weeks: R² 0.85 (HDD only, base 15 °C), 0.94 (with PV and interaction).

Provenance: a source search by a sub-agent on 2026-11-27; every page below was read on that date. Labels: **read** = seen on the page or in the PDF's text; **metadata** = bibliographic data checked on Crossref or an index, content not read; **secondhand** = only as quoted or summarised by another source; **not verified** = not found. Many primary texts (ASHRAE Guideline 14, IPMVP Core Concepts, VDI 3807, most journal articles) are paywalled or blocked automated access. Read by the principal on 2026-11-27.

## For people

- **Verdict: partly proven.** The frame is standard measurement and verification: a regression of whole-site consumption on weather over a baseline period, an "adjusted baseline" predicted for each later period's weather, and savings as "avoided energy" = adjusted baseline − actual (IPMVP Option C; US FEMP guidelines; ASHRAE Guideline 14).
- The HDD with a base temperature fitted to the data is PRISM (Fels 1986) and the "variable-base degree-day" model of ASHRAE's inverse-modelling toolkit. Solar radiation as a second regressor is also published (energy signatures with a solar-gain term).
- **Our own extension:** the site's own PV yield as the solar regressor, the HDD×PV interaction term, and regressing cost instead of energy. No source found uses them. They are plausible, but untested in the literature.
- **Strongest caveat:** the R² values are in-sample. PV and HDD move against each other over the year, so b, c and d are poorly separated, and a week outside the baseline's combination of cold and sun (e.g. a cold, very sunny week) can get a wrong expectation. Check the model out of sample (e.g. leave one season out) and report the saving with an uncertainty band that allows for autocorrelated residuals, as ASHRAE Guideline 14 does.
- Update, 2026-11-27, after this note: the implementation fits grid import and feed-in instead of cost and prices them afterwards, as IPMVP does (section 4.1.8 below), with net cost = import × grid price − feed-in × feed-in tariff. So "cost as the outcome" in section 4 no longer applies; the PV yield as regressor and the HDD×PV term remain the project's own extension. The figures above (R² 0.85 and 0.94) are for the earlier variant; the current ones are in the README, "Comparing control strategies".
- For comparing control strategies, the literature also has a stronger design than before/after: switch strategies at random (e.g. by week) and compare (Raftery et al. 2024, below).

## 1. Measurement and verification with an adjusted baseline

- IPMVP, *Generally Accepted M&V Principles*, EVO, October 2018 ([PDF](https://evo-world.org/images/corporate_documents/IPMVP-Generally-Accepted-Principles_Final_26OCT2018.pdf)), **read**:
  - Routine adjustments: "For any energy-governing factors expected to change routinely during the reporting period (e.g., weather or production volume)", from "a constant value" up to "several multiple parameters non-linear equations each correlating energy with one or more independent variables".
  - Non-routine adjustments: for factors "not usually expected to change (e.g., the facility size, the design and operation of installed equipment, the number of weekly production shifts, or the type or number of occupants)"; their static factors "must be monitored for change throughout the reporting period".
  - Eq. 4: "Avoided Energy Consumption = Adjusted Baseline Energy – Reporting Period Energy ± Non Routine Adjustments". "The adjusted baseline energy is frequently found by first developing a mathematical model that correlates actual baseline period energy data with appropriate independent variables in the baseline period. Each reporting period's independent variables are then inserted into this baseline mathematical model". This is exactly our "expected cost".
  - Option C: the measurement boundary "encompasses the whole facility"; Option C is "the whole facility Option C, defined in Section 3".
  - Baseline period: "should span a full operating cycle from maximum energy consumption and demand to minimum".
  - Section 4.1.7: report "CV{RMSE}, MBE, R2, t-statistic" and "the range of independent variables over which the models are valid". Section 4.1.8: prices are applied to energy savings in the M&V plan; IPMVP models energy, not cost.
  - IPMVP Core Concepts (EVO 10000-1:2022) itself: **not verified** (not opened).
- US DOE FEMP, *M&V Guidelines 5.0*, September 2024 ([PDF](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2024-10/mv_guide_5_0.pdf)), **read**:
  - Section 2.1.2: "Routine adjustments account for expected variations in independent variables ... like weather"; section 2.1.3: they "often use regression analysis".
  - Section 4.4 (Option C): use it only if "Savings are predicted to be greater than about 10% to 15% of the overall consumption", with "At least 12 (and preferably 24 or more) months of pre-installation data", and "Changes to the facility or its use that affect energy consumption are unlikely, or easily quantified". "It is best to develop models using data in whole-year sets (12, 24, 36, or 48 months)".
  - Section 5.3.4.1: "IPMVP states that a minimum acceptable R2 value is 0.75" (IPMVP **secondhand**); "IPMVP specifies that the t-Statistic to be greater than 2.0"; "ASHRAE Guideline 14-2002 accepts an energy model if the net determination bias error is less than 0.005%" (G14 **secondhand**).
  - Table 4-2, "taken from ASHRAE Guideline 14-2023, Section 5.3.3.3.10", for calibrated simulation (Option D): monthly MBE ±5 %, CV(RMSE) 15 %; hourly MBE ±10 %, CV(RMSE) 30 % (G14 **secondhand**).
  - Section 6.16.2 (grid-interactive buildings with PV and storage): recommends Option B and a calibrated simulation, not Option C.
- LBNL for the California PUC, *Site-Level NMEC Technical Guidance* v2.0, 15 Dec 2019 ([PDF](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/files/legacyfiles/l/6442463695-lbnl-nmec-techguidance-01072020.pdf)), **read**: screening thresholds "CV(RMSE) < 25%", "NMBE between -0.5% and +0.5%", "R2 > 0.7" (citing "ASHRAE Guideline 14 and the IPMVP"); the plan must say "how any on-site generation will be treated".
- ASHRAE Guideline 14 (2014, 2023): paywalled, **not read**. Its change-point models (3P, 4P, 5P) come from ASHRAE RP-1050, the Inverse Modeling Toolkit: Kissock, Haberl, Claridge, "Inverse Modeling Toolkit: numerical algorithms", ASHRAE Transactions 109(2), 2003 (**metadata** on the [IIR record](https://iifiir.org/en/fridoc/inverse-modelling-toolkit-imt-numerical-algorithms-22451): "linear, change-point linear, and multiple-linear inverse building energy models"; pages 425–434 only from a reference list). Perez et al. 2017 (below) show "Change-point models for various scenarios as given by ASHRAE" and fit 3P models to daily household data (**read**). No G14 threshold for weekly data found.

## 2. PRISM

- Bibliographic data, **metadata** ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/0378-7788(86)90003-4)): M. F. Fels, "PRISM: An introduction", *Energy and Buildings* 9(1–2), 5–18, February 1986, doi:10.1016/0378-7788(86)90003-4. The publisher page refused access; the abstract was not read.
- Fels, Hurvich, Reynolds, "PRISM research: yesterday, today, and tomorrow", ACEEE Summer Study 1986 ([PDF](https://www.aceee.org/files/proceedings/1986/data/papers/SS86_Panel9_Paper_11.pdf)), **read**: inputs are "meter readings from utility bills and average daily temperatures from a nearby weather station for an approximately one-year period"; output is "Normalized Annual Consumption, or NAC ... a measure of what energy consumption would have been under typical weather conditions"; savings = NAC pre − NAC post; control houses are recommended "to decouple the savings induced by the measures of interest from the savings that would otherwise have occurred due to external events".
- The model form, from Belzer & Cort (PNNL), "Statistical Analysis of Historical State-Level Residential Energy Consumption Trends" ([PDF](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2015/06/f24/belzer_cort_article.pdf), venue not verified), **read**, PRISM itself **secondhand**: E = a + b·HDD(T_rh); T_rh "is defined as the temperature that maximizes the explanatory power of the model above (i.e., minimizes sum of squared residuals)"; "a key feature of the PRISM software is its ability to determine the most appropriate reference temperature". Physically T_rh = T_in − I/(uA), with I the "Internal gains from people, appliances, and solar gain". Our HDD term with fitted T_base is this model. That adding a solar term shifts or flattens the best T_base fits this equation (our reasoning, no source).

## 3. Energy signatures with a solar term

- Rasmussen, Bacher, Calì, Nielsen, Madsen, "Method for Scalable and Automatised Thermal Building Performance Documentation and Screening", *Energies* 13(15), 3866, 2020, doi:10.3390/en13153866 (**metadata** on [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/en13153866); abstract **read** on [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/method-for-scalable-and-automatised-thermal-building-performance-)): peer-reviewed; daily heat use modelled from outdoor temperature, wind speed and global solar irradiation, tested on 16 occupied Danish houses. Equations not read (full text blocked).
- Gondian & Thiery, arXiv:2607.25382, 2026, preprint ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25382)), **read**: Eq. 5, Ê_d = bl + α_h·(θ_h − θ_d − g·I_d)⁺ + α_c·(θ_d − θ_c)⁺, with I_d "typically taken as the average daily global horizontal irradiation" and g "an effective additional coefficient that needs to be calibrated"; attributed to Rasmussen et al. 2020. The solar term sits inside the heating hinge, so sun only lowers consumption on heating days.
- Smertinas, Nielsen, Van Hove, Bacher, Madsen, arXiv:2503.22321, 2025, preprint ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22321v1)), **read**: energy signature with a solar-gain term gA·I_g and a fitted T_base, "the temperature at which the building's heating system switches on the heating", on 2,788 Danish houses.

## 4. PV yield as regressor, HDD×PV interaction, cost as the outcome

- No source found that uses a site's own PV yield as a regressor in a weather-normalisation model, an HDD×solar product term, or cost as the regressed quantity. **This part is our own extension.**
- Why it may be reasonable (our reasoning):
  - Weekly PV yield is a measured, site-specific proxy for irradiance on the house, as I_d is in section 3.
  - The interaction mimics the hinge in Eq. 5 of section 3: summed over a week, sun reduces heating only on heating days, so the solar effect on heating grows with HDD.
  - In a house with PV, PV yield also enters the cost directly (self-consumption at 30 ct/kWh, feed-in at 8 ct/kWh, the pilot's tariff in requirements.md). A cost model without it would blame the strategy for sunny or dull weeks.
- Risks (our reasoning):
  - Collinearity: HDD is high when PV is low. b, c, d are then unstable, even if the prediction inside the baseline's range is good. Check variance inflation factors and the coefficients' t-statistics (IPMVP: |t| > 2, per FEMP).
  - Extrapolation in two dimensions: the 90 %–110 % coverage rule (section 6) applies to each variable, but HDD×PV combinations not seen in the baseline can still be outside it.
  - PV depends on the strategy only if the system curtails. The site has no feed-in limit, so PV is close to independent, but clipping at the inverter, outages or a strategy that stops export would make it endogenous. Check that measured PV follows irradiance (e.g. the satellite data in pv-battery-model.md).
  - Cost instead of energy: IPMVP models energy and applies prices afterwards (section 4.1.8). With today's fixed tariff, cost is a fixed linear combination of import and export, so this is equivalent. A tariff change is a non-routine change; with a variable tariff, model import and export energy separately and price them afterwards.
  - The share of PV self-consumed is what a strategy changes. c and d describe the baseline strategy only, which is right for an adjusted baseline, but they must not be read as a property of the house.

## 6. Pitfalls named in the sources

- Baseline length: a full cycle from maximum to minimum (IPMVP); at least 12, preferably 24 months, in whole-year sets (FEMP). 98 weeks covers two heating seasons.
- Extrapolation: ASHRAE Guideline 14, quoted by the LBNL NMEC guidance (**secondhand**): "Apply the algorithm for savings determination for all periods where independent variables are no more than 110% of the maximum and no less than 90% of the minimum values of the independent variables used in deriving the baseline model."
- Autocorrelation:
  - G14-2014 section 4.2.11 contains a "correlation coefficient ρ" in its savings uncertainty calculations (ASHRAE [errata sheet, 24 July 2019](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20errata/guidelines/gdl14-2014errata-7-24-2019-.pdf), **read**; the equation itself not read).
  - Gondian & Thiery give the G14 approximation with n′ = n·(1 − ρ)/(1 + ρ), ρ the lag-1 autocorrelation of residuals, attributed to Reddy & Claridge 2000 (**secondhand**).
  - Touzani, Granderson, Jump, Rebello, "Evaluation of methods to assess the uncertainty in estimated energy savings", *Energy and Buildings* 193, 2019, doi:10.1016/j.enbuild.2019.03.041 ([LBNL page](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/evaluation-methods-assess-uncertainty), abstract **read**): the standard methods "tend to underestimate the uncertainty", more so for hourly than daily models. Weekly data were not tested.
- Changes in occupancy or use: non-routine adjustments (IPMVP, FEMP above); NMEC: drop a suspected non-routine event if it spans under 25 % of the data, otherwise exclude the building. Keep a log of household changes (guests, holidays, new appliances).
- Savings small against noise: FEMP warns that savings below about 10–15 % may be "lost in the noise" with monthly data.
- Alternative design: Raftery, Zou, Parkinson, Hancock, "Reliably estimating the impact of an active control strategy in a building", *Journal of Building Engineering* 98, 111134, 2024, doi:10.1016/j.jobe.2024.111134 (**metadata**, [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Reliably+estimating+the+impact+of+an+active+control+strategy+in+a+building+Raftery&rows=2)). Its use is **read** in Jayarathne et al., ASHRAE Transactions, January 2026 ([PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt89v4257q/qt89v4257q.pdf)): strategies chosen at random each day so that both "covered all types of days", which "significantly reduce[s] the total M&V period" and mitigates changes "unrelated to the controls being studied".

## 7. The temperature source: Open-Meteo against the heat pump's sensor

Open-Meteo's history is the only temperature source covering the baseline from January 2025, so the model uses it. The heat pump's outdoor sensor (`sensor.altherma_climatecontrol_aussentemperatur`, Home Assistant's hourly means) has been recorded only since 2026-11-21. A first comparison over 126 hours, 21 to 27 November 2026 (by the agent, read-only, 2026-11-27): the sensor reads on average 0.65 K warmer (median 0.77 K, standard deviation 0.99 K, from −2.0 to +3.0 K); +1.0 to +1.6 K at night, −0.7 to +0.2 K around midday (08:00–14:00 UTC); daily means +0.8 to +1.6 K. The principal had expected the sensor a little high, since it sits close to the house; the unit blows its cold air into the garden and draws in air near the sensor, so recirculation is unlikely (principal, 2026-11-27). Five cold days cannot tell a house effect from a known weakness of gridded data in calm, clear nights, nor whether the offset changes with season. For comparing strategies this matters little, as long as all weeks use the same source. Worth repeating after a few weeks of overlap, as a note rather than a plot (principal, 2026-11-27).
